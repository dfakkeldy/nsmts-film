This is Mabou in the 1880s, as Hugh Fletcher surveyed it. And this is Mabou today. I'm Dan Fakkeldy, and I built NS Marks The Spot to put them on the same map.

Nova Scotia publishes a lot of map data, but it's scattered: provincial ArcGIS services, open data portals, federal and municipal servers. I know where it lives, how to query it, and what each licence allows. The map draws on more than a hundred layers, and every one keeps its source and licence on screen.

Pick a parcel and it checks roads, water, buildings, civic addresses and assessments, each against its own source, and it tells you when a source comes back empty.

Georeferencing is the heart of it. Drop in a scan, match a few landmarks, and it drapes onto today's map. Every point shows how well it fits, and which one disagrees with the rest. Hand-drawn maps bend, so a curved warp passes through every point. That's how twenty-four of Fletcher's sheets got onto the map.

It also screens rivers for small hydro, and shows where a proposed wind farm could be seen from, on bare earth.

Frame a map and export a georeferenced PDF. Bring GeoPDFs in too, and see where you are on them.

On a phone, it logs points, photos and tracks. It all stays on the device until you export it.

This one I use at work. Where's that house? Is the driveway too far? There's no signal at work, so I made it run offline. My coworkers use it too. One day I couldn't find an address: not in the list, and no house on the aerial. I know almost every address now. I built it for whoever starts next.

It's also a native iPhone app, in TestFlight.
